**What’s new with FABRIC?**

_An update from Ilya Baldin_

Happy New Year 2022 everyone! The FABRIC team is busy with completing the Phase I infrastructure deployment, which includes installing our core DWDM links between Starlight, Dallas, Salt Lake City and Washington DC sites. We are working with our regional partners at UEN and MAX to turn on the regional DWDM links connecting MAX and University of Utah sites to the core. Additionally, we expect the last of the Phase I edge sites (FIU, UCSD, Clemson, Georgia Tech and GPN) to be deployed soon. They are currently being assembled by our vendor prior to shipping them to their final destinations. 

In February / early March we expect to turn up our completed Phase I topology and turn it over to our early experimenters. At the same time, we are working on the Phase II deployments as well as the Terabit core. We expect our first public IPv6 peering (at Starlight) to be turned on in the same timeframe. 

Finally, we are developing solutions to make P4-programmable FPGA resources and Tofino switches available to our experimenters.

**FABRIC In The News**

_ExoGENI: A critical step forward for edge cloud computing_

Ilya Baldin and his team are drawing on their experiences with ExoGENI to create a next-generation distributed system known as FABRIC. This NSF-funded testbed combines a cloud system with high-speed optical links to give scientists a place to study new Internet architectures at scale. FABRIC will let computer scientists study completely new approaches to storing and processing data on the fly so that they can figure out what might be applicable to the Internet of the future.

“Although FABRIC is an independent project, it is in many ways an intellectual successor to ExoGENI,” said Baldin, who is the PI for the FABRIC project. “Like ExoGENI, FABRIC involves building a system while it is being used—but with ExoGENI we proved that our team can handle taking off in an airplane that’s still being built.”

Read the [full blog](https://renci.org/blog/exogeni-a-critical-step-forward-for-edge-cloud-computing/) online.

**FABRIC: A Testbed of Testbeds**

_Participating Testbeds and Facilities_

FABRIC is a testbed of testbeds, helping users experiment using multiple testbeds. Like Lego blocks, users can get accounts on several testbeds and build an experiment using all of them. Additionally, testbeds can be powered by FABRIC. FABRIC can support testbeds as an underlying infrastructure, while not necessarily exposing the FABRIC interfaces to their users. 

The list of participating testbeds and facilities is growing and currently includes [Chameleon Cloud](https://chameleoncloud.org/), [CloudLab](https://www.cloudlab.us/), [COSMOS](https://www.cosmos-lab.org/), [PEERING](https://peering.ee.columbia.edu/), [Powder](https://powderwireless.net/), and more. Testbeds and facilities can be added to the page using [this form](https://share.hsforms.com/13ryeyx2VRjyaY9Q8kB9Wgg3ry9k).

Find the [full list](https://fabric-testbed.net/resources/testbeds) on our website.

**Defining FABRIC**

_A glossary for common terms used by our researchers_ 

**Resource:** A physical or virtual entity that can be provisioned by the Control Framework. Note that some resources are for use in experiments (experimental resources), and some resources are for measurement use (measurement resources). Experimental and measurement resources can be further divided into different resource types such as GPUs, NIC cards, FPGA, etc.

**Resource Delegation:** Resources made available by AM to a particular Broker via advertisement including a resource advertisement and associated meta-information.

**Resource Reservation:** Resources made available to experimenter by the Broker via the Orchestrator.

See the [full glossary](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1qKf-LhDEjQOME8Pi5aMlmZiKPWfBizos/view) on our website.
