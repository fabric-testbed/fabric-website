# **FPGA-Based Image Processing with Regular Expressions on FABRIC | Stitching Together Innovation with FABRIC Users**  

_June 16, 2026 3:00-4:00 pm ET_ 

![](https://learn.fabric-testbed.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/image-1024x576.png)

Join us tomorrow, June 16, 2026, from 3:00–4:00 PM ET, for a FABRIC webinar featuring an FPGA-based image processing experiment that explores a new, language-based approach to image analysis. In this session, the presenters will provide an overview of the experiment and explain how they are using regular expressions to encode image components such as eyes, noses, and faces for processing on an FPGA-based regular expression engine.

The webinar will also highlight how the experiment was co-developed across the Open Cloud Testbed (OCT) and evaluated on FABRIC. The presenters will discuss their workflow for creating the engine on OCT, transferring the binary, and running evaluations on FABRIC. Attendees can expect a user-led demonstration of the FPGA portion of the work, along with a discussion of the evaluation process on FABRIC.

Whether you are interested in FPGA-based experimentation, image processing, or learning how to evaluate a custom application on FABRIC, this session will offer a look at an emerging approach to research computing and testbed-based experimentation.

# **FABRIC News** 

The FABRIC team announced the winners of the "Stitching Together Innovation" Awards at KNIT12 in Honolulu, recognizing outstanding contributions across education, experimentation, tooling, and research. Nik Sultana received the Best Classroom Use Award for integrating FABRIC into multiple courses over three years, Mazahir Hussain won the Best Experiment Award for demonstrating intent-based bandwidth reservations for exascale science workflows, Alexander Wolosewicz earned the Best Matrix Award for developing a network debugging tool now integrated into Fablib, and Arash Sarabi and Prateek Jain received the Best Paper Award for their Multi-Level Error Detection framework that improves large-scale data transfer reliability. These awards showcase how FABRIC enables innovation from classroom learning to cutting-edge networking research and infrastructure development. Full article can be found here: [https://learn.fabric-testbed.net/knowledge-base/announcing-the-winners-of-the-fabric-stitching-together-innovation-awards-at-knit12/](https://learn.fabric-testbed.net/knowledge-base/announcing-the-winners-of-the-fabric-stitching-together-innovation-awards-at-knit12/)

**Educational Use: Using FABRIC for Class Projects**

As a national research infrastructure for cutting-edge and exploratory research in networking, distributed computing, and science applications, FABRIC encourages faculty to use the infrastructure for teaching their classes. Training next-generation researchers to imagine and construct new computing and networking experiments at scale is an important goal for FABRIC. FABRIC has created sample experiments and Jupyter notebooks that are ready to be used by networking and systems classes for instructors. 

These experiments include basic ping, network routing, exploring IPv6, TCP analysis, traffic generation, setting up a web server, using Ansible to manage a set of nodes, etc. They are available via GitHub as well as the FABRIC Artifact Manager. Of course, you can create your own assignments for students to do on the FABRIC infrastructure. The FABRIC team provides support from creating an education project, enrolling students, to answering questions from instructors and students. More information can be found [here](https://learn.fabric-testbed.net/knowledge-base/fabric-information-for-instructors).
